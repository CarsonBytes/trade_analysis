# LLM veto layer — retrospective (2026-11-21)

**Scope:** the live-trading "LLM veto": a language model (`board_scan`) reads each deterministic
BUY signal and can change it to WAIT. This retro asks whether that veto has helped, using **only
decisions the system already logged** (tables `board_scan_signals`, `board_scans`,
`rejected_signals`, `paper_trades`, `ib_mirror`, `scan_metrics`), with outcomes replayed on real
market prices. No LLM was asked anything about the past.

**Window:** LIVE log 2026-08-18 → 2026-11-20 (814 scans, 9,300 LLM verdicts). PAPER log
2026-08-13 → 2026-11-20 (438 scans, 5,573 verdicts). That is about 3 months, and one bad period
for the strategy.

---

## 0. Bottom line

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Does the veto improve performance? | **No measurable effect, positive or negative.** Over 3 months the portfolio ends up the same with or without it: LIVE −11.93% with the veto vs −11.78% without; PAPER −12.10% vs −11.59%. Shuffling the order of trades that start on the same day moves the result by 5–8 points. |
| Does it pick out the bad trades? | **No.** Signals it vetoed did as badly as signals it approved: LIVE −0.656R vs −0.661R (gap −0.005R, 95% CI −0.32 to +0.20). PAPER −0.681R vs −0.745R. Both accounts pooled: gap +0.016R (CI −0.23 to +0.20). |
| Why was it credited with saving −79.5R? | That number counts each veto as a trade avoided. In practice **the LLM re-approved the same ETF a median 31 minutes later** (LIVE). 84 of 97 LIVE veto episodes would have filled at the same next-day open price anyway. Only **2** LIVE veto episodes were never re-approved. Approved signals, replayed the same way, lost just as much. |
| Is it independent of the other filters? | Mostly not. 84% (LIVE) and 97% (PAPER) of vetoed setups would have been blocked anyway by trend strength, RSI over 70, an already-open position or the re-entry gate. |
| Does LLM confidence predict outcomes? | **Not reliably.** Replayed approvals show a weak positive link (Spearman +0.37 LIVE, +0.23 PAPER). Actually-traded positions show the **opposite** (−0.64 LIVE, n=30; −0.46 PAPER, n=33). Every live funded trade with confidence ≥0.9 lost (5 of 5). |
| Does it get careful in falling markets? | **No.** It vetoed 7.9% of BUY verdicts while SPY was above its 20-week average and **0.0%** below it (LIVE). The 46 BUY episodes it approved below the average all lost. |
| **Verdict** | **Keep it running (it costs about $1 a month and does no measurable harm), but stop crediting it, do not extend it to exits or sizing, and redesign it** so it makes one frozen decision per completed weekly bar. Then the F3 shadow ledger can actually score it. |

## 1. What the layer does, in plain words

1. Each week the deterministic scorer looks at every ETF. If the trend lines up, it says **BUY**.
   This is `score_from_facts`, with gates in `paper.evaluate_signal`.
2. Roughly every 15–60 minutes, the LLM ("board scan") reads the same ETFs plus the news and
   returns its own verdict for each: BUY, WAIT or SELL, with a confidence and a short reason.
3. When the scorer says BUY and the LLM says WAIT, no trade is placed and the row is journaled as
   *"LLM vetoed to WAIT (deterministic was BUY): …"*. This check runs **first**, before the
   trend-strength, RSI, open-position and re-entry checks
   (`dashboard/core/paper.py` lines 765–792). So a veto row says nothing about whether those other
   gates would also have blocked the trade.
4. The LLM is asked again at the next scan. When it says BUY then and every other gate passes, the
   trade is placed.

Point 4 is what matters most for this retro. A single WAIT only blocks a trade if the LLM **keeps**
saying WAIT.

### Important caveats (read before the numbers)

- **The LLM can only be judged going forward.** Asking an LLM today whether it would have bought
  DIA on 2026-08-18 is useless, because it already knows what happened. Everything here comes from
  decisions it logged at the time.
- **Bad period.** Jul–Nov 2026 is a poor stretch for this trend-following strategy. The backtest's
  own signals lost −0.43R per trade then (fidelity F1/F2, HANDOFF). Most BUY signals lost, so any
  filter that says "wait" looks clever on paper. That is why vetoed signals are compared with
  approved signals, not with zero.
- **The two accounts are not independent.** Paper has reused live's LLM scan since 2026-09-17
  (commit `669a20a`) and mirrored it since 2026-10-02 (`84e1966`). In the combined numbers, each
  ETF-week is counted once. LIVE and PAPER never disagreed on an ETF-week (0 of 162).
- **The replay is an approximation.** Real trades entered at the live price and closed on live
  ticks. The replay enters at the next session's open and resolves on daily bars. Compared on the
  30 resolved real LIVE trades, the replay is more optimistic: −0.130R vs the real −0.377R, with a
  correlation of 0.79 and 80% of signs matching. On PAPER (33 trades) it gives −0.317R vs −0.455R
  (correlation 0.61). Both groups are replayed the same way, so the **comparison** is fair; the
  **absolute** R levels are rough.

## 2. Method (how every number was produced)

- **Data:** read-only copies of the two databases.
  - LIVE: `live_ro.db`, a snapshot of `/data/dashboard_live_docker.db` taken 2026-11-21.
  - PAPER: `paper_ro.db`, copied from `/data/dashboard_docker.db` with `docker cp`. The paper
    container had already stopped (exit 143) when I got there, so the planned online `backup()`
    could not run. A stopped container means a consistent file, and its `PRAGMA integrity_check`
    returned ok. Nothing in the container was written to.
- **Decision points:** every `board_scan_signals` row where the scorer said BUY.
  - "Approved" means the LLM said BUY. "Vetoed" means it said WAIT, or 14 LIVE / 10 PAPER times
    SELL, which the long-only rule blocks the same way.
  - The tech ETFs (QQQ, XLK, CSPX, EIMI, ASHR) are left out up to the last
    "tech investment paused" journal row (LIVE 2026-08-20, PAPER 2026-09-17). That pause is checked
    before the LLM.
- **De-duplication.** The same setup is re-checked every scan, so three units are used:
  - **24h streaks:** the same rule as `llm_veto_replay.py`, applied to both verdicts. Per ETF and
    verdict, a new episode starts only 24h or more after the last one kept.
  - **ETF-days:** one per ETF per day. Approved if the LLM approved at least once that day.
  - **ETF-weeks:** one per ETF per ISO week. "Vetoed" only if the LLM **never** approved that week.
- **Outcome replay** (`llm_retro_data.py::replay`):
  1. ATR and trend come from **completed** weekly bars only, i.e. bars whose week had ended by the
     decision time. This avoids the in-progress-bar bug fixed in F1.
  2. Entry is the next session's open.
  3. Stop and target come from `paper.compute_sltp(…, "ATR", rr=3.0)`: stop 1.5×ATR, target 3R.
  4. The trade is resolved on daily bars within 35 calendar days by `paper.resolve`, and R is
    computed by `paper.r_multiple` with the standard half-spread cost.
  5. Episodes whose 35-day window had not finished by 2026-11-20 are left out of the outcome
    statistics.
- **Uncertainty:** confidence intervals come from a bootstrap that resamples **calendar weeks**,
  not single trades. Signals in the same week move together, so resampling single trades would
  overstate certainty.
- **Overlap flags** (would another gate have blocked it?):
  - strength under 5, from the logged `det_strength`;
  - RSI14 over 70, from completed weekly bars;
  - an open "ATR rr3.0" trade on that ETF at that moment, from `paper_trades`;
  - the re-entry gate: the last closed trade on that ETF was a loss and price had not climbed back
    1R above its entry.
- **Regime:** SPY's last completed weekly close vs its 20-week average. It was above every week up
  to the week of 2026-10-19 and below from 2026-10-26 to 2026-11-16 (latest 689.81 vs 756.21).

## 4. Vetoed vs approved outcomes (Q2)

### Replayed R per episode (24h streaks, finished windows)

| | Approved n | Approved avg R | Approved win | Vetoed n | Vetoed avg R | Vetoed win | **Gap (approved − vetoed)** | 95% CI |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| LIVE | 446 | −0.661 | 15% | 97 | −0.656 | 14% | **−0.005R** | −0.317 to +0.203 |
| PAPER | 403 | −0.745 | 10% | 76 | −0.681 | 12% | **−0.064R** | −0.359 to +0.117 |
| Combined (deduplicated) | 503 | −0.659 | 15% | 116 | −0.674 | 13% | **+0.016R** | −0.232 to +0.200 |

A useful veto would show a clearly positive gap: approved trades doing better than vetoed ones. The
gap is zero within noise in both accounts. Outcome mix, LIVE: wins 2% approved vs 4% vetoed;
losses 81% vs 82%; expiries 16% vs 13%.

**Only setups no other gate would have blocked** (strength 5, RSI ≤70, no open position, re-entry
clear):

| | Approved | Vetoed |
|---|---|---|
| LIVE | n=57, −0.236R, 37% win | n=16, −0.077R, 56% win (8 of the 16 are DBC, the period's best performer) |
| PAPER | n=12, −0.880R | n=2, −1.004R |

The sample is tiny. If anything, LIVE's independent vetoes did *better* than its approvals.

### What a veto actually did in practice

| | LIVE | PAPER |
|---|---:|---:|
| Veto episodes (24h streaks) | 97 | 76 |
| Re-approved by the LLM within 35 days | 95 | 76 |
| Never re-approved (a true block) | **2** (both lost, −1.01R each → **+2.0R saved**) | **0** |
| Median time to re-approval | **0.51 h** | **1.16 h** |
| Re-approved within 1 h / 24 h | 72 / 88 | 27 / 72 |
| Re-approval would fill at the **same** next-session open as the vetoed signal | 84 | 61 |
| Replayed R entering at the veto vs at the re-approval | −0.649 vs −0.630 → **+1.8R** total delay benefit (n=95) | −0.681 vs −0.725 → **−3.3R** (n=76) |

Over three months, then, the veto's real effect in replay units is about **+3.8R on LIVE**
(+2.0R from true blocks, +1.8R from delays) and **−3.3R on PAPER**. That is small and goes both
ways. A re-approval does not guarantee a trade was placed, since the other gates still apply, so
this is the most the veto could have done.

### Reconciling with the "−79.5R" headline (HANDOFF line 26)

I re-ran `llm_veto_replay.py` unchanged on both copies:

- LIVE: 96 episodes, 9% win, **−0.828R**, −79.53R;
- PAPER: 76 episodes, 8% win, −0.818R, −62.16R.

The arithmetic is right. Two things make it misleading:

1. **It never replays the approved signals.** Run on approved 24h streaks, the same tool method
   gives LIVE −0.679R (n=463) and PAPER −0.722R (n=421). The gaps are +0.151R and +0.096R, inside
   the ±0.25R noise band above. Nearly everything lost in this period, approved or not.
2. **It treats every veto as a trade avoided.** Nearly all were re-approved within the hour (see
   above).

The tool also has a small look-ahead: it uses weekly bars up to the veto time, including the
unfinished current week, and takes that bar's **final** close as entry. My replay uses completed
weeks and the next open, which moves the vetoed group from −0.83R to −0.66R. Either way, "the LLM
veto is the best filter" is **not supported** and should be corrected in HANDOFF.

## 5. Portfolio backtest: does the veto change the money? (user request)

**Setup.** Logged decisions only, run through the real engine
`dashboard.research.backtest._portfolio(cands, 0.01)` with live settings: `POS_CAP=0.25`,
`PORTFOLIO_CAP=1.0`, cash yield 3%, 1% risk per trade. The engine keeps one position per ETF and
spreads risk across groups.

**Units.** One per ETF per day, with entry/stop/target/outcome from the replay above.

**Arms.**

- **(A)** with the veto: approved ETF-days only.
- **(B)** without the veto: all deterministic BUY ETF-days.
- **(C)** placebo: (B) minus a random set of ETF-days the same size as the vetoed set, 20 seeds.

**Order noise.** `_portfolio` processes trades that start on the same date in list order, and that
order changes the result. So every arm was run with 20 random same-date orderings and the
**median** is reported.

| Arm (median of 20 orderings) | Trades | Total R | Avg R | Win | Final equity | Max drawdown | CAGR* |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| **LIVE (A) with veto** | 74 | −48.2 | −0.652 | 14% | ×0.8807 (−11.93%) | −11.93% | −39.3% |
| **LIVE (B) without veto** | 76 | −50.2 | −0.661 | 13% | ×0.8822 (−11.78%) | −12.08% | −38.9% |
| LIVE (C) placebo, 20 seeds | — | −50.2 | — | — | median ×0.8813 (range ×0.851–×0.916) | −11.87% | — |
| **PAPER (A) with veto** | 71 | −51.7 | −0.728 | 10% | ×0.8790 (−12.10%) | −12.19% | −38.1% |
| **PAPER (B) without veto** | 72 | −52.8 | −0.733 | 10% | ×0.8841 (−11.59%) | −12.59% | −36.8% |
| PAPER (C) placebo, 20 seeds | — | −52.8 | — | — | median ×0.8608 (range ×0.843–×0.913) | −14.00% | — |

\*CAGR stretches 93–98 days to a yearly rate. It is shown because it was asked for, but at this
length it means very little.

**How much does the veto matter?**

- **A − B, final equity:** LIVE −0.15 points, PAPER −0.51 points of equity. Total R is +2.0R and
  +1.0R better with the veto; max drawdown is 0.15 and 0.40 points shallower.
- **A vs placebo:** A beats 40% (LIVE) and 70% (PAPER) of random removals of the same size. That is
  what removing trades at random would also give.
- **Order noise:** arm A alone ends anywhere from ×0.826 to ×0.903 (LIVE) or ×0.863 to ×0.914
  (PAPER) depending only on same-day ordering. That is roughly **10–50 times larger** than the A−B
  difference.
- **Other units give the same answer.**
  - ETF-days that pass strength ≥5 and RSI ≤70: LIVE A ×0.9022 vs B ×0.8921, a +1.0-point gap with
    only 2 vetoed days, inside the order noise. PAPER: identical, since no vetoed days remain.
  - ETF-weeks: LIVE identical (no vetoed weeks); PAPER A ×0.8893 vs B ×0.8959.

**Conclusion:** over the logged window, the veto changed portfolio results by less than the noise
from trade ordering. "By how much" is: indistinguishable from zero, and smaller than a random
filter's spread.

**How much data would settle it?** Per-setup R has a standard deviation of about 0.91R. To detect a
real gap between approved and vetoed setups with 80% power at the usual 5% level, you need about:

| Gap to detect | Independent setups per group |
|---|---:|
| 0.5R | ~52 |
| 0.3R | ~143 |
| 0.2R | ~322 |

Today there are **2** truly blocked setups (LIVE). At the current October–November veto rate, a
confident verdict would take years. That is why the redesign in section 9 comes first.

## 6. Does the LLM's confidence or bias predict outcomes? (Q3)

### Approved ETF-week setups, replayed R by confidence bucket

| Confidence | LIVE n | LIVE avg R | LIVE win | PAPER n | PAPER avg R | PAPER win |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| < 0.70 | 32 | −0.727 | 6% | 24 | −0.508 | 13% |
| 0.70–0.80 | 44 | −0.891 | 7% | 49 | −0.845 | 6% |
| 0.80–0.90 | 47 | −0.581 | 17% | 43 | −0.673 | 16% |

| ρ comparing within each ETF | +0.231 | | | −0.013 | | |
| ρ comparing within each week | +0.204 | | | −0.031 | | |

**On trades actually taken, the sign flips:**

| Trades | n | Spearman (confidence vs realized R) | Detail |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| LIVE resolved ATR trades | 30 | **−0.643** (p<0.001) | conf ≥0.8: 11 trades, all about −1.0R; conf <0.8: 19 trades, about −0.01R |
| PAPER resolved ATR trades | 33 | **−0.462** (p=0.007) | conf ≥0.8: 8 trades, all about −1.0R |

**Reading:** the positive link in the replay comes largely from which ETF the LLM is confident
about. DBC and QQQ got high confidence and held up best; SHY got low confidence and stayed flat.
Within a single ETF or a single week, the link weakens to about +0.2 on LIVE and disappears on
PAPER. On real fills it reverses. With n of about 30 heavily overlapping trades, none of this can
justify sizing positions by confidence.

**Bias carries no information.** Every approved setup was "bullish" (142/142 LIVE, 133/133 PAPER),
as were 29 of 30 real LIVE trades. WAIT verdicts are low-conviction: median confidence 0.62 (LIVE)
and 0.61 (PAPER), against 0.80 for approvals.

## 7. Is the LLM adding independent information? (Q4)

Share of vetoed setups (24h streaks) that another gate would have blocked anyway:

| Overlap | LIVE (n=97) | PAPER (n=76) |
|---|---:|---:|
| trend strength < 5 | 37 | 38 |
| RSI14 > 70 (overextended) | 29 | 24 |
| already holding the ETF | 21 | 31 |
| re-entry gate | 15 | 20 |
| **any of the above** | **81 (84%)** | **74 (97%)** |
| independent of all other gates | 16 | 2 |

For comparison, **approved** ETF-weeks are equally covered by other gates: 85% LIVE (123/145) and
96% PAPER (131/136). So the high overlap does not show the LLM agreeing with the filters. Most
scorer BUYs in this period are blocked by *something*. The LLM's vetoes are no better aimed at
setups the filters miss than its approvals are.

This repeats the July 2026 audit in HANDOFF, which found the LLM's caution redundant with the RSI
filter (31/31 episodes confirmed). Note also that `llm_gate_audit.py`'s first check searches for
the string "no confluence". The veto label changed on 2026-07-13, so that check now always reports
0 and is out of date. Its episode check finds LIVE 15 of 16 and PAPER 15 of 16 trend episodes
LLM-confirmed at the start.

RSI is rebuilt from completed weekly bars. Before F1, live used the unfinished bar, so a few RSI
flags may differ from what live saw.

## 8. Does it depend on the market regime? (Q5)

| | SPY above 20-week average | SPY below 20-week average |
|---|---|---|
| Weeks in window | through week of 2026-10-19 | 2026-10-26 → 2026-11-16 |
| LIVE veto rate (BUY scans) | **7.9%** of 6,756 | **0.0%** of 510 |
| PAPER veto rate | **10.1%** of 3,087 | **1.1%** of 348 |
| LIVE approved episodes | 400, −0.621R, 17% win | **46, −1.008R, 0% win** |
| LIVE vetoed episodes | 97, −0.656R (86% would have lost) | none |
| PAPER approved episodes | 356, −0.711R | **47, −1.008R, 0% win** |
| PAPER vetoed episodes | 72, −0.663R (88% would have lost) | 4, −1.006R |

The concern was that vetoes might cluster in falling markets, where saying WAIT is easy. **The
opposite happened.** Vetoes came in the rising Aug–Sep market, where vetoed and approved setups did
equally badly. During the late-October to November sell-off, where every approved BUY lost, the
LLM approved everything. Its veto shows no sense of market regime.

## 9. Verdict and recommendations (Q6)

**Verdict: keep running, stop crediting, do not extend, redesign before judging again.**

1. **Keep it, as a harmless and cheap layer.**
   - `scan_metrics` shows about **$0.98** of LLM spend on LIVE from 2026-09-16 to 11-20 (505 real
     calls) and $0.10 on PAPER.
   - There is no measurable harm (portfolio A−B −0.15 / −0.51 points, inside the noise) and no
     measurable benefit.
   - Removing it would also be defensible. But it is the system's only forward-looking news check,
     and the cost of keeping it is negligible.
2. **Correct the record.**
   - HANDOFF line 26 ("The LLM veto is the best filter (96 vetoed signals would have lost −79.5R)")
     should be revised. In the same replay, vetoed signals lost no more than approved ones, and
     almost all were re-approved within the hour.
   - `llm_veto_replay.py` should also replay the approved signals, use completed weekly bars and a
     next-open entry, and report time to re-approval.
   - `llm_gate_audit.py`'s "no confluence" check should match the current veto label.
3. **Redesign (call it "tighten", but really "make it a decision").** Today the veto is a coin flip
   re-tossed every 15–60 minutes, so it only delays entries.
   - Since F1, signals use **completed weekly bars**. Ask the LLM **once per setup per completed
     weekly bar** and freeze its verdict until the next bar.
   - Alternatively, require WAIT to hold for a wall-clock period, such as the full trading day,
     before it counts. HANDOFF already warns against counting scans because the cadence varies.
   - Only then does a veto actually remove a trade, and only then can it be measured.
4. **Do not loosen it into sizing or exits.**
   - Confidence does not reliably predict outcomes: the replay shows +0.23 to +0.37, real trades
     show −0.46 to −0.64, and all five live trades at ≥0.9 lost.
   - Bias is always "bullish".
   - An exit rule built on WAIT was already tested and retracted in July: WAIT is an entry
     conviction call, not an exit signal.
5. **Evidence required before extending it** (tie to the planned **F3 shadow ledger**). The ledger
   should log, for every scorer BUY, going forward:
   - the frozen per-bar LLM verdict and confidence;
   - which other gates fired;
   - the exact entry, stop and target that would be used;
   - the outcome at 35 days.

   From that it scores R saved (vetoed trades that lost) and R missed (vetoed trades that won).
   Extend the LLM to sizing or exits only if all of these hold:
   - **(a)** at least about **143 independent vetoed setups and 143 approved** (enough to detect a
     0.3R gap), or about 52 per group if you only care about a 0.5R gap;
   - **(b)** the approved-minus-vetoed gap has a week-clustered 95% CI above zero;
   - **(c)** the edge holds in **at least two regimes**, including a stretch with SPY below its
     20-week average;
   - **(d)** a portfolio replay through `_portfolio` beats at least 90% of same-size random-removal
     placebos, with same-date order shuffled.

   For confidence-based sizing, add a within-ETF Spearman of at least +0.2 whose CI excludes zero,
   **on real fills**.
